Why two people hear the same alarm differently

Hearing isn’t equal across pitches. High-frequency hearing loss—typically described as loss in the 2,000–8,000 Hz range—is common, and research notes it becomes evident from around age 40. Walls and bedding also absorb high frequencies more than low ones. So the same tone can be loud to one partner and faint to the other.

What research says about which sounds wake people

In a study of hard-of-hearing adults, a low-pitched 520 Hz square-wave signal woke 92% of participants at 75 dBA, compared with 56% for a high-pitched alarm. Separate research found alarms at or above 4,000 Hz were often missed. Those studies looked at waking people, not at keeping a partner asleep, but they show pitch matters a lot.

How to test alarm tones with your partner

  1. Set the phone where it will sit overnight, at the volume you’ll actually use.
  2. While you’re both awake, play candidate tones and each say what you hear.
  3. Shortlist tones the person waking hears clearly and the other barely notices.
  4. Try the best one on a real, noncritical morning with a backup alarm set.

Keep volumes moderate during testing.

How Couples Alarm’s tone test works

Couples Alarm turns that test into a guided flow. It only suggests a bundled tone when the person waking first can hear a tested tone the other partner does not.

  1. Choose who needs to wake first for this schedule.
  2. Each partner completes one guided listening turn.
  3. If the first comparison is close or unclear, both complete one confirmation turn.
  4. Preview the suggested tone together and run the real bedside alarm check.
  5. Both partners confirm before scheduling anything.

When a tone won’t be enough

If your partner is a very light sleeper or the test finds no suitable tone, a sound-free route is better: a watch or band haptic alarm, or vibrate-only. Unlike those, Couples Alarm keeps a real sound and needs no wearable—when it fits. Compare options: how to wake up without waking your partner and alarm apps for couples compared.

Frequently asked questions

Can an alarm wake one person and not the other?

Sometimes, because people hear frequencies differently. Testing tones together is the only way to know for your household.

Why does my partner sleep through the alarm?

Common reasons include differences in high-frequency hearing, sleep depth, and where the phone sits. This is not a diagnosis; ask a professional about hearing concerns.

What alarm sound is hardest to sleep through?

In one study of hard-of-hearing adults, a low 520 Hz square-wave tone woke far more people than a high-pitched alarm.

Will Couples Alarm ring if my iPhone is on silent?

Yes. It uses Apple’s AlarmKit, so alarms ring even when the iPhone is silenced.

Is a tone test safe for hearing?

Keep volumes moderate while testing.
