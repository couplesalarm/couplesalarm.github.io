Why one of you can hear it and the other can’t

How high a pitch you can hear varies from person to person. It shifts with age, noise exposure and plenty else. When the two of you differ, there is a band of pitch that one of you notices and the other doesn’t.

This only works if the person who needs to wake up can hear a sound their partner can’t. You’ll test that together during setup.

Five steps, one phone, both of you

  1. 1

    Choose who wakes first

    Tell the app who needs the alarm and who should keep sleeping. Those roles stay fixed through every step that follows.

    • Wakes first
    • Keeps sleeping
  2. 2

    One listen each

    Pass the phone back and forth. A sweep plays through the phone’s own speaker and each of you stops it the moment you hear it. If your answers are close, you both take one confirmation turn.

    • Phone speaker only
    • No headphones
  3. 3

    Preview the tone together

    Couples Alarm suggests a built-in tone only when the person waking first can hear a tested tone the sleeping partner does not. Then you pick how it sounds.

    • Heartbeat hum
    • Escalating
    • Breathing
    • Motor purr
  4. 4

    Run the bedside fit check

    Put the phone where it will sit overnight. A real alarm rings, and each of you answers separately. Setup finishes only when you both confirm the result.

    • Where you sleep
    • Both confirm
  5. 5

    Set your alarms

    Create one-time or weekly alarms with labels and snooze. On iPhone they ring like a built-in alarm, even on silent.

    • One-time or weekly
    • Pick your days

  • If no tone fits, the app says so.It stops instead of guessing.
  • Keep a backup alarm.Use one you trust until your tone passes the bedside fit check.
