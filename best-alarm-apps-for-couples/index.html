How we compared

We checked the U.S. App Store listings and linked developer pages on October 15, 2026. We compare the wake-up method, extra hardware, stated Silent Mode behavior and payment model. These are developer-reported features; we have not independently tested the competing apps. Prices and availability can change. A missing Silent Mode statement is not evidence that an app fails in Silent Mode.

Couples Alarm

View Couples Alarm on the App Store

A two-person tone test to find a sound the person waking first hears and their partner doesn’t—no wearable, just the iPhone. It only suggests a bundled tone when the test finds one.

Couples Alarm schedules its alarms with Apple’s AlarmKit, so they ring like a built-in iPhone alarm, even on silent. If the requested schedule has no suitable bundled tone, the app says so—keep using an alarm you trust for that schedule.

Partner responses and alarms stay on the iPhone. No account or backend is required. Requires iOS 26 or later. Setup and the real fit check are free; completing setup starts one calendar month of full access, followed by a $9.99 one-time purchase for continued full access—not a subscription.

How the tone test works: finding an alarm tone only one of you hears.

Silent Wake

Its U.S. App Store listing describes headphone-only sound, vibration and normal speaker modes, plus wake-up tasks. Headphones are needed for the headphone mode, not the vibration mode. The listing does not explicitly confirm Silent Mode behavior for every mode.

Free download with in-app purchases. Listed offers include $4.99 weekly and annual offers from $29.99 to $49.99; check the purchase screen for the offer available to you.

Apple’s built-in Clock app also sounds through the iPhone speaker when headphones are connected. That is a separate behavior from Silent Wake’s advertised headphone mode; test the app, connection and snooze behavior you plan to use.

VibeAlarm

The U.S. App Store listing describes six vibration patterns, an Apple Watch app with alarm sync, and a Watch-only alarm option. It requires iOS 26.4 or later. A Watch is optional for phone use; the listing does not explicitly confirm every iPhone Silent Mode scenario.

Free download with in-app purchases: $1.99 monthly, $18.99 annually, or $24.99 lifetime in the checked U.S. listing. Pro unlocks all patterns and unlimited alarms.

Sleep for Two — beta invitations

The developer’s website currently asks visitors to request an iPhone beta invitation. It describes alarm timing estimated from bedtime and fixed-length sleep cycles, rather than measured sleep stages. Individual features use one iPhone; shared features use two linked iPhones. No wearable is required.

The site says it is free to try and one subscription covers both partners, but does not publish a price. We could not verify a public App Store listing. Treat it as a beta option, with availability and alarm behavior to confirm with the developer.

Sleep Cycle

The U.S. App Store listing describes sound-based sleep analysis and a Smart Alarm intended to wake you during lighter sleep. No wearable is required for its phone features. That does not establish that your partner will remain asleep or confirm all Silent Mode behavior.

Free download with optional Premium subscriptions. The listing shows several offers, including $57.99 Premium, without a duration beside each price; check the in-app offer for its billing period and renewal price.

Not an app: smartwatches and vibration bands

If you already wear an Apple Watch to bed, consider its built-in haptic alarm. Enable Silent Mode and leave “Break Through Silent Mode” off if that option is shown. Dedicated vibrating bands are another option, with their own charging and setup requirements. More detail: how to wake up without waking your partner. Which should you choose?

  • No wearable, phone only: Couples Alarm.
  • You already sleep with a watch: its built-in silent alarm, or VibeAlarm.
  • You’re fine sleeping in earbuds: Silent Wake.
  • You want sleep tracking and a smart wake window: Sleep Cycle.
  • You want to explore coordinated estimated wake windows: check Sleep for Two’s beta availability.

Whatever you pick, keep a trusted backup alarm until you’ve tested it where you sleep.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alarm app for couples?

It depends on your setup. For a phone-only option that tries to wake one partner with sound, Couples Alarm is built for that; with a watch, a haptic alarm may be simpler.

Is there an alarm that wakes one person but not the other?

Haptic alarms on a watch or band, earbud alarms, and Couples Alarm’s tone test all aim for that. None can guarantee it; test first.

Do third-party alarm apps work when the iPhone is on silent?

Behavior depends on the app and its permissions. Couples Alarm uses Apple’s AlarmKit, which supports alarms that break through Silent Mode. Test the exact app and settings you plan to use.

Do I need an Apple Watch to wake up silently?

No. A vibrate-only iPhone alarm, a band, or Couples Alarm’s tone test don’t need a watch.

Can both partners use different alarms in the same bed?

Yes. See our different wake times guide.
